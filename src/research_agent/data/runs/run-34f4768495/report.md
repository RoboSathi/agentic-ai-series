# Health impact comparison of white and brown bread

**Status:** PARTIAL

## Recommendation

Compared with refined grain, whole grain tends to be absorbed slowly with a relatively low GI [E1]. Refined grain is abundant in carbohydrate content, which leads to a higher dietary GL [E1]. Whole grain is rich in dietary fiber, trace minerals, and phytochemicals [E2]. These nutrients and food components were considered to be beneficial for MetS [E2]. The nutrient components of refined grain were lost during the refining process [E2]. Specifically, nine studies were related to whole grain consumption, and the overall multi-variable adjusted RR demonstrated that whole grain consumption was inversely associated with MetS (RR = 0.80, 95%CI: 0.67–0.97; P = 0.021) [E3]. With regard to refined grain consumption, 13 studies were included. The overall multi-variable adjusted RR indicated that refined grain consumption was positively associated with MetS (RR = 1.37, 95%CI: 1.02–1.84; P = 0.036) [E4]. The summary RR (95% CI) for high vs. low whole grain intake was 0.74 (0.59–0.93, I2 = 97%, pheterogeneity<0.001, n = 9) [E5] and per 90 g/d was 0.86 (0.82–0.90, I2 = 63%, pheterogeneity=0.008, n = 8) [E6]. The summary RR (95% CI) for high vs. low refined grain intake was 0.94 (0.88–1.01, I2 = 7.9%, pheterogeneity=0.36, n = 5) [E7] and per 90 g/d was 0.97 (0.93–1.02, I2 = 0%, pheterogeneity=0.42, n = 4) [E8].
