# Impact of Agentic AI and Coding Agents on Software Jobs

**Status:** PARTIAL

## Summary

Agentic AI is an advanced AI form focused on autonomous decision-making and action, capable of goal-setting, planning, and task execution with minimal human intervention [E1][E5][E6][E7][E8][E9][E10][E12]. Coding agents and agentic AI are reshaping software employment: job listings for software engineers increased 11% annually, outpacing overall postings [E13]; companies are expanding software budgets and engineer headcounts [E14]; software developer employment is projected to grow 15% by 2034 [E15]; and IBM is tripling entry-level US hiring, shifting roles from routine coding to customer-facing AI-specified features [E16]. Meanwhile, AI agents now autonomously resolve over 70% of software issues, up from under 20% a year prior [E17], and 30–40% of code in leading repositories is AI-written [E18]. AI replacement risk scores place software developers among the highest-risk occupations [E19], with the most AI-exposed engineers earning a 47% premium but facing structural disruption risk [E20]. A productivity–experience paradox exists: 84% report productivity gains, yet worsened developer experience nearly doubled from 14% to 27% [E21][E23]. Supervisory engineering work—direction, evaluation, and correction of AI output—is emerging as a new category [E22]. Only 9 of 30 agents provide system cards, and performance benchmarks are reported by a minority [E24][E25].

## Concepts

Agentic AI refers to AI systems with autonomous software agents that reason, plan, and take multi-step action toward goals with limited human input [E1]. Five characteristics define enterprise agentic AI: autonomy, tool use, memory and persistence, proactive planning, and orchestration [E3]. EY defines agentic AI systems with four key components: planning and reasoning, and tools [E4]. Agentic AI is built on perception, reasoning using a large language model, planning, action, and reflection, forming a continuous cycle of perception, planning, action, and reflection that allows agentic AI to learn and improve over time [E8]. Modern systems are defined by proactive planning, contextual memory, sophisticated tool use, and adaptation based on environmental feedback [E9]. AI agents are self-contained autonomous systems designed to accomplish goals, defined by autonomy, proactivity, and end-to-end task completion [E11]. Agentic AI synthesises autonomy and agency through task initiation, dynamic goal ranking, progress monitoring, and feedback loops parallel to human executive functions [E12].

## Examples

Evidence from 2025–2026 shows agentic AI adoption accelerating: Gartner predicts 40% of enterprise applications will embed task-specific AI agents by end-2026 [E2]. EY’s four-component framework (planning, tools, memory, orchestration) [E4] and Springer’s five characteristics [E9] illustrate the technical profile. Only ChatGPT Agent, OpenAI Codex, Claude Code, and Gemini 2.5 Computer Use provide agent-specific system cards among 30 indexed agents [E24], and only 9 of 30 companies report benchmark performance [E25].
